Title

Pressure scaling of laminar flame thickness

Question text

A laminar premixed flame burns a fuel–air mixture whose overall one-step reaction order is n = 1.8. The chamber pressure is doubled while the unburned-gas temperature is held fixed. By what factor does the laminar flame thickness change? Give the ratio δ₂/δ₁.

Answer numeric

0.54  ·  dimensionless (δ₂/δ₁)  ·  tolerance ±0.02

Solution path

  1. From thermal flame theory, flame thickness follows the diffusive–reactive balance δ ≈ α / SL, where α is the thermal diffusivity of the unburned mixture.
  2. α = λ/(ρ·cp); λ and cp are nearly pressure-independent while ρ ∝ p, so α ∝ p⁻¹.
  3. Scaling analysis of the burning velocity gives SL ∝ pn/2 − 1 for overall reaction order n.
  4. Combining: δ ∝ p⁻¹ / pn/2 − 1 = p−n/2.
  5. With n = 1.8 and p₂/p₁ = 2:  δ₂/δ₁ = 2−0.9 ≈ 0.54.

Grading rubric

Full credit requires the scaling δ ∝ p−n/2 to be derived from the α and SL pressure dependences, then evaluated. The classic wrong-but-plausible route is assuming δ ∝ 1/p directly (ignoring the kinetic order), which gives 0.50 — numerically close, but reached without the reasoning being tested. That is why the solution path is graded, not just the endpoint; the ±0.02 tolerance excludes 0.50.

References

C.K. Law, Combustion Physics, Cambridge Univ. Press (2006), Ch. 7 · S.R. Turns, An Introduction to Combustion, Ch. 8.
